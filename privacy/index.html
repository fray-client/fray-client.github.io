Privacy Policy

Effective 12 November 2026 · Fray Client

#The short version

  • Fray has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. The only thing the Fray Client team receives is anonymous usage statistics: which Fray and Minecraft versions are used, how fast they start, FPS test results, what kinds of crashes happen and which features get used. They are on by default, never include your name, account, servers, worlds or files, and one switch in Settings > Privacy turns them off.
  • Your sign-in stays on your PC. Your Microsoft and Minecraft sign-in tokens are stored in one file, encrypted by Windows for your Windows user only, and are only ever sent to Microsoft and Mojang.
  • Fray talks to the services a launcher needs: Microsoft and Mojang (sign-in, game files), Modrinth and CurseForge (mods), mod loader sites (Fabric, Quilt, Forge, NeoForge), GitHub (Fray updates), the Minecraft servers you add, and, if you use it, the Discord app on your PC.
  • Logs and crash reports stay on your PC unless you choose to send them to someone.
  • We will never sell your data, and we never use it for ads.

The rest of this page explains each of these in detail. It describes what the Fray launcher and the Fray mod actually do in version 1.0.0. If Fray changes how it handles data, this page changes first.

#Who we are

Fray is a free launcher for Minecraft: Java Edition, made by the Fray Client team. "We", "us" and "our" on this page mean the Fray Client team.

Email us at frayclient@gmail.com. We read every message, including privacy requests and security reports, and reply from that address.

It reads those folders only when you open Import, and it never reads or copies another launcher's sign-in data.

#Signing in with Microsoft

Minecraft: Java Edition requires a Microsoft account. When you sign in:

  1. Fray opens Microsoft's own sign-in page in a private window. You type your password into Microsoft, not into Fray, and Fray never sees or stores it. The window keeps no cookies after it closes.
  2. Microsoft gives Fray a sign-in code, which Fray exchanges for tokens with Microsoft, Xbox Live and Minecraft's services (login.microsoftonline.com, user.auth.xboxlive.com, xsts.auth.xboxlive.com, api.minecraftservices.com). Fray only asks Microsoft for permission to sign in to Xbox Live and to stay signed in.
  3. Fray reads your Minecraft profile (name, UUID, skin and capes) and, if you don't have a profile yet, whether your account owns Minecraft.

Your tokens are saved encrypted in accounts.dat and are only sent to Microsoft and Mojang: to refresh your sign-in, to start the game, and to change your skin or cape when you ask Fray to. Signing out removes the account from Fray.

Skins and capes. If you upload a skin, Fray sends the image file you picked to Minecraft's services. Skin and cape images you see in Fray are loaded from Mojang's texture servers.

#How the game and the launcher talk

The Fray mod's title menu lets you switch accounts without leaving the game. For this, Fray listens on your own PC only (127.0.0.1, never the network) and gives each game it starts a new random secret. Only a game Fray started, holding that secret, can ask Fray for the list of your accounts or for the sign-in of the account you pick. This connection never leaves your computer.

#Discord status

If the Discord app is running on your PC, Fray can show "Fray Client" on your Discord profile with what you are doing (for example "Idle in Launcher" or "Playing Minecraft 1.21.11", the version's mode and how long you have been playing). Fray hands this to the Discord app on your PC through Discord's local connection; Discord then shows it to people who can see your profile, under Discord's own policy.

This is on by default. Turn it off any time in Settings (Discord status) and Fray clears it right away.

#Logs, crash help and Fray Doctor

  • Crash explanations are made on your PC by reading the game's own crash report. The report and logs are never uploaded; with usage statistics on, only the kind of crash is counted (see below).
  • Fray Doctor checks your PC (memory, graphics drivers, antivirus, folders, recent crashes, connections) on your PC.
  • "Bundle my logs" in Fray Doctor creates a zip in %APPDATA%\.fray\doctor and shows it to you in File Explorer. It contains Fray's recent launcher logs, your Fray settings and versions list, the chosen version's latest game log, options, recent crash reports and mod list, plus a short summary of your PC (processor threads, memory, graphics drivers). Before anything goes in the zip, Fray removes anything that looks like a sign-in token. Fray never sends this zip anywhere. If you share it with someone, read it first: logs can still contain your Minecraft name, server addresses and folder names that include your Windows user name.
  • FPS tests run on your PC. With usage statistics on, the resulting numbers are counted (see below); nothing else about the test is sent.

#Minecraft itself

Fray starts the real Minecraft, downloaded straight from Mojang. Minecraft has its own data collection, which Microsoft runs under the Microsoft Privacy Statement. Fray does not add to it, read it or turn it off. The Fray mod changes how Minecraft looks and loads; it does not send data anywhere except the local connection to the launcher described above.

#What Fray doesn't do

  • No Fray account, no ads, no tracking pixels, no fingerprinting, no advertising IDs. The only data the Fray Client team gets is the anonymous usage statistics below.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your data, with anyone, for anything.
  • No reading of your files outside Fray's folder, the Minecraft folder and, when you use Import, other launchers' game folders.
  • No sending of your sign-in to anyone other than Microsoft and Mojang.

#Usage statistics

Fray sends anonymous usage statistics so we can see which versions people actually play, how fast Fray starts them, how well they run, and what breaks, and fix the most common problems first. They are on by default (like Discord's), Fray tells you about them the first time it opens, and you can turn them off at any time with one switch in Settings > Privacy. That page also shows the last events Fray sent, exactly as they were sent.

What is sent:

  • A random statistics ID, made on your PC (fray\stats-id). It lets us count installs without knowing who anyone is. It is not your Microsoft or Minecraft account, and it isn't linked to them.
  • About your PC and Fray: Fray's version, your Windows version, your processor model and number of threads, your graphics card model and its memory, and how much memory your PC has (rounded to whole gigabytes). Also whether this is Fray's first start, how many game versions are installed and how many accounts are signed in (the number only).
  • Each game session: the Minecraft version, its loader (for example Fabric) and mode (Fray mode, vanilla or a modpack, and for a Modrinth modpack its public Modrinth project ID), whether the Fray mod ran, how many seconds the game took to open its window and reach the title screen, whether it reached it, whether Fray's launch cache or Warm Start was used, whether it was an FPS test run, whole minutes played, and whether it crashed.
  • Crashes: only the kind of crash: one of a fixed list (for example "out of memory", "a mod is missing a mod it needs", "graphics driver", "hang") or the name of the Java error (for example java.lang.NullPointerException). Never the crash report, log lines, file paths, mod file names or your Windows user name.
  • FPS tests: the average FPS, the 1% and 0.1% lows, the render distance, the setting being compared, which run of a Tune it was, how long that run took to start, the version tested, and the same processor, graphics card and memory details as above (so results can be compared by hardware).
  • Features: how many times parts of Fray were used, as counts from a fixed list (for example "searched mods: 4", "installed a modpack: 1").
  • When: the time each of these happened.

What is never sent: your Minecraft name or UUID, your Microsoft or Xbox account, sign-in tokens, the servers you join or add, your worlds, chat, screenshots, skins, file or folder names, your Windows user name, other programs on your PC, or anything you type.

Who receives them. Statistics go to PostHog (PostHog, Inc.), an analytics service that stores them for us on its EU cloud (eu.i.posthog.com) and processes them only on our behalf. Fray asks PostHog not to look up a location and not to build a profile of a person from these events, and our PostHog project is set to discard IP addresses, so your IP address is not stored with them. Like any connection, PostHog's servers see your IP address while receiving the data. Only the Fray Client team can see the statistics, and only as totals and charts (for example "how many installs played 1.21.11 this week" or "average time to the title screen").

How long they're kept. PostHog keeps the events for up to one year, then deletes them. Statistics Fray couldn't send yet (for example while you're offline) wait in memory and are dropped when Fray closes.

Turning them off. Settings > Privacy > "Send anonymous usage statistics". Off means Fray sends nothing more, drops anything not sent yet and deletes your statistics ID. Because the statistics can't be tied to you, we can't find or delete the ones already sent from one particular PC; they are deleted when the one-year limit runs out.

Builds without statistics. A copy of Fray built without a statistics key (for example a test build) never sends anything, whatever the switch says. Settings > Privacy says so.

#Children's privacy

Fray is used by many younger players, so statistics are built to contain nothing that identifies anyone (see above), and we don't ask for, collect or receive names, contact details or other personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

On top of that, Fray doesn't send usage statistics at all while a Microsoft child account is signed in on the PC. When you sign in, Fray asks Xbox Live for the account's age group (Adult, Teen or Child), which Microsoft sets for accounts in a family group. If any signed-in account is a Child account, statistics stay off and the switch can't be turned on. The age group stays on your PC and is never sent anywhere.

Your Microsoft account, including its age and family settings, is managed by Microsoft; if a family setting blocks online play, Fray shows that message and doesn't try to get around it. If you are a parent and have a question about your child and Fray, contact us (see "Who we are") and we'll help.

#Your choices and rights

Almost everything stays on your PC, so you are in control of it:

  • See it: Settings > About > "Fray's folder" opens everything Fray stores.
  • Delete it: sign out of an account to remove it, or uninstall Fray from Windows' Apps list and choose to delete Fray's data. Your worlds in .minecraft\saves are kept either way.
  • Limit it: turn off usage statistics in Settings > Privacy; turn off Discord status; leave Diagnostic logging off.

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EU and UK, or state laws like the CCPA in California), you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal data a company holds about you. The usage statistics we hold are anonymous and can't be tied to a person, so in practice there is nothing about you for us to look up, but you can always contact us and we'll answer.

When you email us, we receive your email address and what you write. We use it only to answer you, never add you to any list, and delete the conversation when it's no longer needed.

#Security

Sign-in tokens are encrypted on disk with Windows DPAPI and are removed from logs and log bundles. Fray's updates are checked against Fray's signing key before they install, so a tampered download is rejected. No system is perfect: keep Windows up to date and don't share your Fray folder or accounts.dat with anyone. If you find a security problem, please tell us privately first (see "Who we are").

#Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, we update the date at the top of this page and say what changed in Fray's in-app "What changed" notes. If a change affects how Fray handles your data, it will be published here before any Fray version that does it.
